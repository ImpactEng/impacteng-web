AWS reworded its Bedrock abuse-detection page

Between 11 and 15 October 2026, AWS's Amazon Bedrock abuse-detection page dropped the sentence that Bedrock does not store inputs or outputs by default. The before and after, what did not change, and the check for anyone who quotes it.

Dated
15 Nov 2026
Thread
Engineers working with AI
Read
4 min, 846 words
Opening

If your policy, risk register or client answer says "Amazon Bedrock does not store prompts by default", it is quoting a sentence that, since mid-October 2026, is no longer on the AWS page it came from.

I relied on that sentence myself in a LinkedIn post about running AI privately, published on 12 October. By 15 October, when I needed it for another post, the page had been reworded. I don't know if anything changed in how Bedrock handles data. But I can see that the page changed.

Section 01

How I know what it said before

The page is "Amazon Bedrock abuse detection" in the Amazon Bedrock User Guide (docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/abuse-detection.html). I have dated copies of the page:

  • 2 October 2026: a saved copy, from testing my own Bedrock setup. I caught the change cause I had it.
  • 11 October 2026: the key sentence, quoted in a post draft that evening.
  • 15 October 2026: another copy, compared line by line with the 2 October one.
  • 14 November 2026: another copy, for the later update below.

So the change happened between 11 and 15 October. The Bedrock User Guide's document history didn't list it on 15 October, and on 14 November it still had no entry for an October change to the page.

Section 02

What changed

2 October 202615 October 2026
Default storage"Amazon Bedrock uses a zero data retention (ZDR) data security model. This means that by default, Amazon Bedrock does not store model inputs or outputs."Not on the page.
Operator access"Amazon Bedrock uses a zero operator access (ZOA) data security model. This means no operators of the service can access model input or output.""Amazon Bedrock uses a zero operator access (ZOA) data security model, subject to the model-specific abuse and CSAM detection detailed below."
OpeningAbuse detection "may use automated abuse detection mechanisms".Adds: "In certain limited circumstances described below, we may also store or review model inputs or outputs."
Model-specific storage"we may be required to store inputs and outputs""we will store inputs and outputs as follows"
OpenAI modelsTen models named."all OpenAI models other than GPT OSS Safeguard 120B, GPT OSS Safeguard 20B, gpt-oss-120b, and gpt-oss-20b"

The child sexual abuse material section was also edited: it now covers image "inputs or outputs", says Bedrock "may block" rather than "will block", and is shorter.

Section 03

What didn't change in October

  • For Claude Fable 5 and 5.1, "all traffic will be retained for up to 30 days for automated offline abuse detection", with flagged traffic subject to possible human review by AWS, and a programme offering zero retention to eligible customers "through December 31, 2026". Same wording on both dates. This part changed in November (below).
  • Retained inputs and outputs "are not shared with third-party model providers".
  • Under cross-Region inference, retained data is stored in the Region where the request is processed.
  • The separate "Data retention" page, which holds the setting you control per Region, still says: "There is no data retention change to Claude models released before Claude Fable 5." (as at 15 October 2026)
Section 04

What changed again in November

As at 14 November 2026, everything I quote from the October version above is still on the page, and the sentence about not storing inputs or outputs is still gone. A later update, which the document history dates 9 November 2026 and which lists the "Data retention, Abuse detection, and Quotas pages" among those updated, changed the Fable lines:

  • The 30-day retention now covers "Claude Fable 5, Claude Fable 5.1, and Claude Fable 5.2": "Amazon Bedrock retains all traffic for up to 30 days for automated offline abuse detection."
  • The zero-retention programme for eligible customers ran "through December 31, 2026" in October. It now offers zero data retention "for internal use through at least June 30, 2027".
Section 05

How I read it

The new page describes storage case by case: named models, and child sexual abuse material detection. It no longer makes the general statement that nothing is stored by default. If your own documents relied on that general statement, I'd point to the data retention setting itself instead: the mode you have set in each Region, and the "Data retention" page that defines it.

Section 06

A quick check

  1. Search your policies, risk registers and client answers for "does not store", "zero data retention" or "ZDR" next to a Bedrock link.
  2. For each one, re-read the page it cites, write down the date and save a PDF.
  3. Check the data retention mode you've set, in every Region you call. The "Data retention" page says the setting "is per-Region: it governs every request in the Region where you set it and does not propagate to other Regions." (as at 14 November 2026)

I keep dated copies of my sources, but that doesn't replace clearer, more transparent changes from providers.
