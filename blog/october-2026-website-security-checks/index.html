Is your website caught up in October's security problems?

Ten minutes of checks for Australian and New Zealand small businesses: who issued your certificate, whether your site runs PHP, what to ask your host, and whether you still need the site at all.

Dated
25 Oct 2026
Thread
Small business websites and security
Read
6 min, 1,265 words
Opening

I worked on PHP and LAMP sites years ago, so I’ve seen both sides of PHP. It’s a big part of why so many small businesses could get online cheaply. It’s also why one flaw can reach so many of them at once.

October brought one of those flaws, plus two certificate problems. There have been plenty of headlines and not much plain advice for the people who actually own these websites. So here’s what I’d check if I ran a small business or a community group in Australia or New Zealand.

Most of you will find you’re fine. It takes about ten minutes, and you don’t need to be technical. Facts are as at 25 October 2026.

Section 01

Check 1: who issued your website’s certificate?

Your certificate is what puts the padlock in a visitor’s browser. Two certificate problems came up this month, and one has a deadline of 27 October.

To see who issued yours, go to ssllabs.com/ssltest (free, run by the security company Qualys), tick “Do not show the results on the boards”, and enter your web address. After a minute or two (if you see a list of servers, click any one), look at the certificate’s “Issuer” and “Revocation status” lines. “Good (not revoked)” is what you want to see.

If you see What it means What to do
Starfield or Go Daddy, and the status says revoked GoDaddy revoked about 380,000 certificates this month because the paperwork behind them didn’t match GoDaddy’s own published rules. Nothing was hacked, but a revoked certificate no longer works properly Log in to GoDaddy and reissue and install the certificate, or ask whoever set up your site
Starfield or Go Daddy, not revoked Not affected. GoDaddy has finished revoking every certificate caught up in this Nothing to do
emSign, or EM DV, EM OV or EM EV That certificate company’s signing keys were weak enough for anyone to recover. Chrome has already blocked the worst of them, and on 27 October (US time, so 27 or 28 October here) it stops trusting the whole emSign ECC Root CA - G3 family. The company has since said it is leaving the website certificate business Get a replacement from a different certificate company now, before 27 October
Anything else Neither problem applies to you Nothing to do

When I checked on 25 October, neither Australia’s ACSC (Australian Cyber Security Centre) nor New Zealand’s NCSC (National Cyber Security Centre) had published anything on either certificate problem. Both have published on PHP.

Section 02

Check 2: does your website run on PHP?

On 21 October PHP got an urgent fix for two flaws that let someone take over a website without a password. Attackers started trying them on 17 October, before the fix was out. As at 25 October, no successful break-ins had been confirmed, and Australia’s ACSC said it knew of none here, but this is the one to act on.

Your site almost certainly runs PHP if it’s built with WordPress (WooCommerce shops included), Joomla, Drupal or Magento, or if your hosting account has a cPanel or Plesk control panel. If it’s on Squarespace, Wix or Shopify, the platform runs the servers, so this one is their job.

If your site does run PHP, you almost certainly can’t fix it yourself. Your host can, and most will already be on it. What you want is a clear answer in writing. This is the email I’d send:

Subject: PHP security update for CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619

Hi, my website [your web address] is hosted with you. PHP released urgent security fixes on 21 October 2026 for CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619. Could you please confirm in writing:

  1. Which PHP version runs my website, and does it include the fixes for these two flaws?
  2. On what date was the update installed and PHP restarted?
  3. If my website runs an older PHP version (8.1 or earlier), what is your plan for it?
  4. Did you check my website for signs of a break-in before you installed the fix?

Keep the reply, and don’t judge it by the version number alone. Hosts often fix older-looking versions without changing the number. Ubuntu’s fixed build, for example, still reads 8.3.6, which looks a long way behind PHP’s own fixed 8.3.36. If your control panel shows PHP information, the build date is a better sign: Hostinger tells customers that a build dated 21 October 2026 or later has the fix, and VentraIP also points to the build date.

If your plan is a “VPS” or “unmanaged”, your host probably expects you, or whoever set it up, to do the update. GoDaddy, Hostinger, SiteHost and 1st Domains all say so for those plans.

If you use WordPress, one more check. The company behind the UpdraftPlus backup plugin was broken into over the weekend of 17 and 18 October, and attackers used its remote-management connections to add admin accounts and plugins to some customers’ sites. If you connected your site to UpdraftCentral, its cloud dashboard: log in to WordPress directly, update UpdraftPlus to 1.26.13 (free) or 2.26.13 (Premium) or later, remove any administrator or plugin you don’t recognise, and change your passwords.

Section 03

The bigger question: do you still need this website?

This is the part I’d spend the most time on. Every website has to be kept up to date, forever. If yours isn’t earning its keep, this is a good moment to decide.

Three questions: how many real people visited in the last three months (ignore huge “hits” numbers, which are mostly bots); did any enquiries, bookings or sales come through it; and does it do something nothing else does, like an online shop, bookings or member logins?

Your answers Option
Real visitors, and it takes orders, bookings or logins Keep it. Get the host’s written answer above and keep plugins up to date
Real visitors, but it’s just information about you Simplify it. Turn it into plain pages with no PHP behind them, which several services host for little or nothing, or move it to a site builder that does the updating for you
Hardly anyone visits, but you want a record Archive it. Save a copy, take it offline, and leave one page with your contact details
Nobody visits and it does nothing for you Retire it, after the checks below

Before you cancel or move anything:

  1. Check where your email lives. Many small businesses’ email runs on the same hosting as the website. Ask your host whether yours does before you cancel anything.
  2. Keep your domain name on auto-renew, even if the website goes. A lapsed domain can be bought by someone else and used to pretend to be you.
  3. Forms and bookings need a new home if the site becomes plain pages.
  4. Save a copy first. Your host can make a full backup.
Section 04

Other things this month

  • Windows: make sure the October 2026 update is installed (Settings, then Windows Update, then Update history). Attackers are already using one of the flaws it fixes.
  • Your backups: if a company backs up your business, ask whether their service runs on AhsayCBS and whether they’ve installed the October hotfix.
  • If an IT company looks after your network: ask whether they use Fortinet, Palo Alto, BeyondTrust or Ivanti products for you, and whether those have this month’s updates.
Section 05

If you think you’ve already been hacked

Call before you delete anything. In Australia: the Australian Cyber Security Hotline, 1300 CYBER1 (1300 292 371), or IDCARE’s free small-business helpline, 1800 595 170. In New Zealand: the NCSC on 0800 114 115 (free, 7am to 7pm, Monday to Friday), or report online at ncsc.govt.nz/report.
