Business owner Fix and automate

Questions I hear, and what I do about it

Can I get more customers?

I set up simple tracking that shows which posts, ads and referrals bring enquiries, so you know where to spend time.

Example: Your website's enquiry form tells you which post or ad each enquiry came from.

What am I getting for what I pay?

I list every tech bill, what it does and who uses it, and mark what to cancel, combine or keep.

Example: Three subscriptions nobody opens, and two tools doing one job.

Can I spend less time on paperwork and chasing people?

I automate the steps you repeat: reminders, follow-ups, and moving details from one system to the next.

Example: Clients get a reminder for missing documents, and it stops when they upload them.

How do I avoid being scammed or hacked?

I set up the basics against common attacks: two-step sign-in, email that's harder to fake, and tested backups.

Example: Your email set up so messages faked from your address get rejected.

Enterprise leader Advise and align

What I have learnt, and what I do about it

Small early results win more backing than big plans

I start with one small result you can show, then build the plan on it.

Example: One assessment finding, fixed by your team and measured, before the roadmap goes up.

Dates hold when the people delivering set them

I build the roadmap with the teams who will deliver it, so the dates are theirs.

Example: Each milestone has an owner in the team, and they set its date.

Approvals go faster when risk and security join early

I bring architecture, security and risk in from the first draft, so approval is not a late surprise.

Example: Security reviews the design before the build, not after it.

Change sticks when business and technology own one plan

I tie business strategy to engineering strategy, so both sides work from one plan.

Example: One plan the business and technology leaders both sign.
