Lookalike Domain Check

Free, in your browser

Is anyone using a name that looks like yours?

Type your business domain. I try common slips and near-copies of it and tell you which are already registered, and which of those have mail servers set up.

One domain at a time, such as yourbusiness.com.au. You can paste a web address or an email address and I will pick out the domain. I do not take lists. It makes up to 300 lookups (600 requests if Google does not answer and each is asked again at Cloudflare) and usually takes under a minute. Each name I try is sent to Google Public DNS and Cloudflare DNS, which are the two services that answer.

Fake invoices can come from names like these. Write a one-page rule for bank detail changes, so your team knows what to do.

How this works

What gets looked up

  • I make up to 150 variations of your name: a missed, doubled or neighbouring-key letter (a typo), two letters swapped, lookalike characters such as "rn" for "m", "vv" for "w", "1" for "l" or "i", "0" for "o", a hyphen added, and other endings (.com.au, .au, .com, .net.au, .net). I use ordinary letters and digits only, not lookalike letters from other alphabets, so a lookalike of that kind will not show here.
  • For each one, your browser asks Google Public DNS, and Cloudflare's as a backup, for two public records: the address record (A), which says a name has a website address, and the mail exchanger record (MX), which says which servers take its email. That is two lookups per name, so at most 300 lookups in all and, if Google does not answer, each is asked again at Cloudflare, so at most 600 requests. Six names are checked at a time, one lookup per name at once, and each lookup gets 5 seconds in all, shared between the two services. A Cancel button stops it.
  • A name counts as registered if either record answers. A name registered with neither will not show here. A "null" MX record, which says a domain takes no email (RFC 7505), does not count as mail. A name that could not be checked is counted and mentioned, never listed.
  • Unregistered variations are not shown, on purpose. A list of names nobody has taken yet would help the wrong person.
  • I never open the registered domains. They appear as plain text, not links. For a name that is not on the list of shared endings I know (such as com.au, net.au, co.uk and co.nz), or shaped like one, I take the last part as the ending. If you type one of those endings on its own, or a free hosting address such as github.io, I ask you for your own domain instead.
  • I do not store the domain you type or the result. Every name I try goes from your browser to the two DNS services, and nowhere else.

Limits

What this does not do

This reads your public DNS records. It is not a security audit or a penetration test.

It does not log in to anything, send email, or visit your website. It cannot tell who owns a registered name or why, and it does not try every possible lookalike.
